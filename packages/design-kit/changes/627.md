A stepper is wrong exactly where the browser's constraints say its field is, so its own error and
its form's refusal agree.

- **Upgrading.** A page that relied on a blank stepper showing its error without `required` adds
  `required` to the stepper, as `<StepperField required />` or on the input. A stepper left blank
  that is not required shows no error and its form saves.
- **Controls.** `attachStepper` reads the field's `validity` to decide whether its own error shows:
  a value outside the limits, between steps or that is no number, and none at all only in a field
  that is required, where a blank showed the error in any stepper while its form saved. It follows
  `required` as a script changes it, as it does its limits, and clears an error whose value a script
  has since made right as its form is submitted.
- **Docs.** The reference's stepper section says when a stepper is wrong, that one whose blank has
  no meaning takes `required`, that a stepper that fails untouched takes its form's error and words,
  and what stays as it was, and its lead stepper example is required. Record 21 says the stepper's
  own error follows the same constraints.
- **Tests.** Browser tests in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox, in a form attached with `attachForm` and
  one refused through `useFormRefusal`, save a blank stepper that is not required with no error,
  refuse a required one emptied and one holding 2.5 with the stepper's own error, refuse one holding
  a letter with one error, and give a required stepper drawn blank and never touched the form's
  error. They find the error gone as a script makes the field optional, and gone on a submit after a
  script set the value right.
