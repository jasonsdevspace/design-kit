A stepper's field keeps its stepper inside it in a row of the page's own.

- **Sheets.** A `dk-field` holding a `dk-stepper`, not inline, is never narrower than the stepper
  with its buttons at their least width, wherever the page's row leaves its items their own least
  width, so in a page's own flex row beside a text field the stepper stays inside its field. The
  stepper alone sets that width, so a long word, a picture or words that never wrap in the label,
  hint or error leave the field as wide as its stepper. A stepper's button is the target wide at
  most, its edge included, in place of a width, so it asks for the target where there is room and
  for its least width where there is not, and a thicker edge a page gives it no longer widens it.
  The field's least width rests on `:has()`, and without it the field keeps none.
- **Docs.** The reference says a stepper's field with its label above is never narrower than the
  stepper at its least width where the page's row allows it, that the stepper alone sets that width,
  that a row whose items have no least width can still squeeze it, that one with its label to the
  left keeps its least width too, and that in a row that cannot wrap the fields beside it take what
  is left. Its section on fields says a row of the page's own lets its fields wrap, with
  `flex-wrap: wrap`, a gap of `--dk-space-4` both ways and a basis such as `flex: 1 1 7rem` on a
  text field, so at a large text size each field takes its own line. The accessibility notes add the
  stepper's field to what Firefox 120 draws without `:has()`.
- **Tests.** Browser tests draw a stepper field beside a text field in a page's own flex row at 320
  wide, at the usual and twice the text size, and find the stepper and both its buttons inside the
  field, at least 44 wide and the target tall. Another gives the field a hint with words that never
  wrap and finds the field no wider than its stepper's least width, with the stepper and its buttons
  inside it.
