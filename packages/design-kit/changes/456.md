A stack can space what it holds at a pair's or a heading's step, and drops a list's markers.

- **Upgrading.** A `ul` or an `ol` marked `dk-stack` loses its markers, its indent and its own room, so a page that keeps them puts the stack around the list rather than on it, and one that leaves them off adds `role="list"`, since VoiceOver in Safari drops the list's role with its markers.
- **Sheets.** Written beside `dk-stack`, `dk-stack-pair` spaces what the stack holds at `--dk-space-2`, for a label over its value or a header's lines, and `dk-stack-heading` at `--dk-space-3`, for a heading over what it heads, each by setting `--dk-flow-step` on what the stack holds, in `tokens.css`. A named step holds between everything the stack holds, under a title and a page section's heading too, a stack inside one takes its step unless it names its own, and a hidden piece adds no step. A stack on a `ul` or an `ol` drops the markers, the indent and the list's own room, and a list nested in it keeps its own.
- **Docs.** The reference has a Stacks section, drawn from a new example of a label over its value and a round's heading over its scores. It says a stack on a list carries `role="list"`, since VoiceOver in Safari drops the role with the markers, an `ol` whose numbers matter puts them in its words, a group with a heading needs no named section, scores compared across rounds are a table, and the step between a page's sections stays the page's.
