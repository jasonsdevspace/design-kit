A control outlives a takedown and an attach of its element in the same task, so a hook's ref made afresh on every render, a ref that merges two hooks and an element passed from one hook's ref to another's keep their control, a form's check and a busy wait included, as record 23 has it.

- **Upgrading.** An abort now takes a control down a microtask later rather than at once, so a call made in the abort's own task, such as a panel's `set` or a dialog's opener, still acts. A page that aborts a control and attaches the same element again in one task now gets the same control back, with its state and the parts it found first, rather than one attached afresh. A page that means to start afresh calls the function the attach returned, which still takes the control down at once, or attaches after a microtask. A dialog attached again with options only for its `signal` drops `stay`, as a later call with options from its newest holder does, so a page that attaches a dialog through a ref made afresh gives `stay` every time.
- **Controls.** Each signal an attach of an element is given holds its control, and the control is taken down a microtask after the last of them aborts, unless the element is attached again by then, which joins it and keeps its state. A control attached again returns what its first attach returned and runs on the options of its newest holder, the one before taking over as the newest lets go. A toast taken from the page with focus in it before its takedown lands hands focus back, in Chromium, which fires focusout as it goes, as in WebKit and Firefox. A copy button joined with the same source keeps the word it shows, and progress counts its steps again only where they change.
- **React.** The hooks take their part down at once, their ref's cleanup aborting the element's signal and dropping it in one call, and a ref called with null does so for every element. `useRoving` and `useProgress` pass their new options with the element's own signal, so they apply to that hook where two share an element. `StatusProvider` keeps no controller of its own.
- **Docs.** Record 23 states the rule and why deferring the takedown in the hooks was turned down, and records 13, 15, 20 and 22 say what it changes. The reference's section on taking a control down stands on its own, names `moveFocus`, says when a takedown lands and how options follow the newest holder, and each attach links it once. The doc comments on `./controls` and the hooks say what the code does now, and a panel's button taken from the page without being taken down is said to stay held until it is.
- **Tests.** The helper's unit tests find an abort's takedown waits a microtask, a signal held before then keeps the control, a call with no signal holding only where nothing else does, the detach taking it down at once with no listener left on any signal, and the newest holder's options applied as holders come and go. Browser tests in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox abort and attach every piece in one task and find each kept with its state, attach after a microtask and find each afresh, hand focus back from a toast taken down and removed in one task, and join each piece again and again with nothing growing and nothing left once it is taken down. Under `StrictMode`, a form, a row of cells and a busy button behind refs made afresh keep their check, their observer and their wait, a busy wait and a shared row keep through a merged ref, a form and a row passed between hooks take the new hook's options, and either of two form hooks dropped leaves the other's check and refusal. Tests that called a piece in the abort's task now call it once the takedown lands, and the tests of a later signal held while the first aborts now find the control kept.
