In Chrome a change to a page restyles what it touches, where every element or word added restyled
the whole document.

- **Upgrading.** A page that writes a shown toast into a live app's frame by hand, with `data-shown`
  and no `attachToast`, writes `data-toast-rows` on the frame, its `dk-app` element, too, with the
  value of the toast's `data-rows` or with no value for two rows, since the body keeps room under
  the toast by that mark. A toast shown through `attachToast`, and a picture of a frame, need
  nothing. A dialog on a page with an app's frame, or one that ends in a tab bar, is written inside
  the frame, or before or after it as its sibling. One a page writes apart from its frame, such as
  one it portals to the body beside a wrapper that holds the frame, takes the class `dk-dialog-app`.
  Without it such a dialog is drawn as a content page's above 48rem wide, with controls at the
  field's height, the wider inset and its actions at its end.
- **Sheets.** No rule writes a `:has()` on the root, the body, an app's frame or a page that ends in
  a tab bar unless it draws that element, since Chromium restyles everything inside an element whose
  `:has()` draws something else. The body is the subject of its own rule for the padding under a
  page's toast, and a body that is itself the frame takes none. An app's frame keeps the room under
  its toast by `data-toast-rows` on the frame, and a pictured frame by the shown toast it holds. A
  dialog takes an app's sizes where it sits inside a frame or a page that ends in a tab bar, beside
  one, or carries `dk-dialog-app`. On a page of about 2,300 elements in Chromium 158, an element
  added restyles 10 elements in 0.14 ms where it restyled every one in 12 ms, and words said on a
  status line take 0.06 ms where they took 6 ms. Showing a toast restyles the whole page once where
  it did twice. The room in a frame, and a dialog inside or after a frame, hold in a browser with no
  `:has()`. A picture of a frame with a shown toast inside a live frame leaves the live frame's body
  as it is.
- **Controls.** `attachToast` writes `data-toast-rows` on the app's frame its toast sits in while
  the toast floats over the frame's body, with the rows it measured or with an empty value for a
  toast it could not measure, and takes it off where the toast takes its row above the dock, as the
  toast is dismissed or taken down, and from a frame the toast has left. The mark is on the frame,
  so it lasts through a body the page draws afresh. A toast in a frame whose body is not the frame's
  own child, inside a form for one, takes its row above the dock. It writes each attribute only
  where its value changes.
- **Docs.** The reference's sections on the app's frame, the toast and the dialog say what marks the
  frame, what a page that writes a shown toast by hand writes, with its markup, and where a dialog
  is written on an app's page and when it takes `dk-dialog-app`. Record 27 gives the measurements,
  the rule and the options turned down, and records 13, 15 and 22 name the mark.
- **Tests.** A browser bench in Chromium reads the elements each recalculation restyled from the
  browser's trace on a content page, a frame and a page that ends in a tab bar, and holds an element
  added, words on a status line and a field marked in error to a twentieth of what a change to the
  root's font size restyles, and showing a toast to no more than one restyle of the whole page.
  Browser tests in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox find the frame marked while a toast floats over its
  body and unmarked in its row above the dock, after each way of dismissing it, after a takedown and
  on a frame the toast has left, the room kept through a body replaced by a new element and inside a
  body that is itself the frame, a toast in its row where the body sits inside a form, and a dialog
  beside a wrapper that holds a frame at an app's sizes with `dk-dialog-app`. In Chromium and WebKit
  they find a dialog at an app's sizes after, before and inside a frame and at a content page's set
  apart from one, and in Chromium and Firefox the room in a frame kept with every selector that asks
  for a `:has()` taken out. A sheet test fails a `:has()` on an element that holds the page in a
  rule that draws something else, and holds every list of an app's places to one form.
