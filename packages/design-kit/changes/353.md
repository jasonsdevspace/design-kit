Every tab in a row stands on a line when the row wraps.

- **Sheets.** A resting tab draws the rule along its foot as an edge, as the chosen tab draws its
  accent line, and a row's tabs stand a pixel lower on the row's rule, so a row too wide for its
  place wraps onto more lines with every tab on a line, in forced colors and on paper too. On one
  line a resting tab's edge and the row's rule are one, the chosen tab's line lies over the rule as
  it does on a container's rule, and the row is a pixel shorter.
- **Docs.** The reference's tabs say a row that wraps keeps every tab on a line, and that
  `dk-tabs-on-rule` suits a container whose rule sits right under the row, so a bar where something
  can wrap under the row lets the row draw its own rule.
- **Tests.** A browser test wraps a row of tabs at twice the text size and finds a line at the foot
  of each tab on every line, against the page's ground, in both themes and forced colors. The tabs'
  test holds a resting tab to the rule's line.
