A view's heading takes focus on arrival only when a visitor's press on a button or a link, or a submit, brought the view.

- **Controls.** `focusOnArrival(heading, { from })` moves focus to a view's heading as it arrives, as `moveFocus` does, only where the visitor's last input was a press on a button or a link, or a submit, that no view has taken focus for. A page's first draw, a key that moves, such as Tab or an arrow along a row of tabs, typing, a press on nothing and a press in a row of tabs take nothing, and Enter or Space on a page's own control that is a button or a link by its role counts. Views drawn by one press land together and the first in the page takes focus. The module now notes inputs with one listener on the document as it loads in a page, so the press that brings a page's first view is heard, and a request called off before it lands hands its press back.
- **React.** `useArrivalFocus` decides through `focusOnArrival`, so it no longer takes focus from a row of tabs, after the visitor only tabbed or typed, or long after an unrelated press, adds no listener as it draws again, and calls its request off as its view leaves.
- **Docs.** The reference says the hook is for a view a press brings, as a tab bar's, that a row of tabs' panels take none, and names `focusOnArrival` for a page without React. Record 20 says the hook keeps only its key and leaves the decision to `./controls`.
- **Tests.** Browser tests arrow along a row of tabs after a press that brought no view, choose a tab by a press and by Enter, draw a view late after a Tab press and after typing in a field, send a view away in its first frame, bring a view and a section of it with one press, and count the document's listeners before and after many draws.
