A page can take down the theme switch and the copy button by aborting a `signal`, the first attaches to keep the teardown contract record 22 writes down for every attach.

- **Controls.** `attachThemeSwitch(element, { signal })` and `attachCopyButton(button, { source, status, signal })` take an `AbortSignal`, and aborting it does what the function they return does, the switch's listener on the document taken off with it however its choices read. A signal already aborted does nothing, so the control stays hidden, and a control already attached keeps the options it had. A press the clipboard answers after the teardown says nothing, and a control attached again before it is taken down does not hear the later call's signal. The theme's listeners on the window stay, so `chooseTheme` still follows another tab with no switch on the page. The type `AttachOptions` carries the option. Both take themselves down through one internal helper, which no entry exports, which runs every undo even where one throws and reports the error.
- **Docs.** Record 22 states the teardown contract, with the research and the options weighed, and record 13 is amended for it. Record 13 sets the controls' budget at 17.75 KB, from 17.25 KB, once the helper and the two attaches moved onto it took them to 17,532 bytes with a median function of 586 bytes. The reference says how a control is taken down, with a screen's controller aborted before the next screen attaches, and gives the two attaches their `signal`.
- **Tests.** Browser tests in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox attach the theme switch and the copy button with a signal already aborted and find nothing attached and a repeat attach's new source not taken, abort a signal and find both hidden, find a late clipboard answer and a theme chosen after the teardown change nothing, find an old detach called twice and its signal aborted leave a newer attach alone, and find a later attach's signal unheard. In Chromium they count the listeners on the controls' own elements and on the document, and find every one the attach added off after an abort. Unit tests hold the helper to running once, whichever of the abort and the detach comes first, to running every undo where one throws, and to taking its listener off the page's signal.
