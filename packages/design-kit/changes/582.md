A toast lets focus fall to the page's body in every engine where nothing else takes it, and sends it
on to the control inside a frame of the page's own origin that had focus there as its message came.

- **Controls.** `attachToast` takes off a message's shown state, then hands focus back, then hides
  its buttons, so the control focus goes to is not scrolled clear of a toast that is leaving, and
  where nothing it tries takes focus, it moves focus off the toast, so focus falls to the page's
  body in WebKit and Firefox as it does in Chromium, rather than staying on the toast's hidden
  button. Where a page's own listener moves focus elsewhere as the toast, a dialog or a panel hands
  it back, the handback goes no further down its list and that focus stays, and a `moveFocus` asked
  before a handback that focused nothing leaves the page's focus alone. Focus a place hands on to
  its shadow tree, as a host that delegates focus does, counts as handed back, while a place that
  refuses focus is passed over though focus is inside it, so a `moveFocus` whose target is a section
  with no tabindex goes on to its fallbacks. A dialog lists what the browser handed focus to as it
  closed last, so where its opener has gone, a `moveFocus` asked in the task that closed it still
  lands. Where the handback focuses a frame of the page's own origin that had focus as the latest
  message came, it goes on to the control inside that had focus there then, and stays on the frame
  where that control is gone, marked off, on a page the frame has since replaced. A frame of another
  origin keeps focus itself, as near as a page can reach, though Firefox does not focus such a frame
  while a key is being pressed, so a dismiss by keyboard there goes on to the body of the app's
  frame or the main content.
- **Docs.** The reference's toast section and its section on taking a control down say where focus
  goes where nothing takes it, where a page moves it during a handback and where it goes back to a
  frame, of the page's own origin or another. Record 23 gives the controls' new weight, and the
  reference says the main content takes focus by the `tabindex="-1"` the skip link's markup puts on
  it.
- **Tests.** Browser tests in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox find focus on the page's body as a press
  on a toast's action is handled where nothing it would hand focus to takes it, and focus left where
  a page's listener moves it as focus leaves the action, with the main content able to take focus or
  not, and a focus request asked before the dismiss leaving it there. They find a host that
  delegates focus taken as handed back, so a request asked before the dismiss lands, and the scroll
  left where it was as focus goes back to a control in view, on a page and in an app's frame. They
  find focus sent on to the button inside a frame of the page's own origin that had focus as the
  message came, there too after a click on the frame's empty space, focus left on the frame where
  that control is marked off, gone, or on a page the frame has since loaded, and focus on a frame of
  another origin after a press by pointer. In Firefox, tests find a dismiss by Enter sending focus
  to the main content where that frame cannot take it, and focus left on the frame's other button
  where a page's listener moves it there during the handback. In every engine, a focus request goes
  on to its fallback where its target holds focus but refuses it, and one asked in the task that
  closes a dialog lands where its opener has gone and the browser handed focus elsewhere.
