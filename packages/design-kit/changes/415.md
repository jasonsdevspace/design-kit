A panel closed after a visitor's press inside it hands focus back, however it was closed.

- **Controls.** Safari and iOS take focus off what a pointer or a tap presses and leave it on what
  holds it, the body or a main that takes focus. The kit keeps the path of a visitor's last press,
  and a panel that closes while focus sits on what holds it, after that press was inside the
  panel, moves focus to the button that opened it. So a Done button of the page's own that calls
  `set(false)` keeps the visitor's place, even where its handler waits for a save first or draws
  the button away. Focus anywhere else, where the visitor or the page put it, stays where it is,
  so a page that moves focus to a field before closing keeps it there, and closing an outer panel
  and an inner one with one press leaves focus on the outer panel's button.
- **Docs.** The reference's corner Close and the controls' doc comments say so, and record 13
  names the rule and the ones turned down.
- **Tests.** Browser tests close a panel by a Done of the page's own, pressed as the browser
  presses it and as Safari leaves focus, one that waits, one that draws itself away and one that
  moves focus outside first, close an outer and an inner panel with one press, close one panel by
  a press in another, have the page's script click the Close with focus inside, and close a panel
  from the page's code after the visitor moved on. A WebKit test holds that a pointer press leaves
  a button unfocused there, so the tests of Safari's way keep testing it.
