A panel's focus on closing follows one rule, decided by where focus is and where the visitor's last
focus or press was, and Escape closes a panel.

- **Upgrading.** The kit writes `tabindex="-1"` on an attached panel and on the place a link in it
  sends focus to, and `tabindex="0"` on its button and its corner Close, where the page set none,
  and keeps a tabindex the page set. A page's own `[tabindex]` and `:focus` rules match these, and
  in Safari the panel's buttons become stops for Tab. Escape in a panel now closes it, so a piece of
  the page's own in a panel that takes Escape for itself calls `preventDefault` on the press.
- **Controls.** However a panel closes, focus goes to its button where focus is in the panel, or
  where nothing has focus and the visitor's last place, the last focus that moved or their last
  press, was in it, and stays anywhere else, so a page that moves focus to what it shows as a panel
  closes keeps it there. Where focus goes back and the visitor's last press was a link in the panel
  to a place on the same page outside it, focus goes to that place. Escape with focus in a panel or
  on its button closes the innermost such panel and marks the press handled, so a dialog around it
  stays open, unless the page has handled it or focus is on a search with words in it or on
  anything else marked expanded, such as a combobox with its list open.
- **Docs.** Record 19 states the rule, the research behind it and the options turned down, and
  record 13 measures the controls with it in place. The reference's corner Close and the
  accessibility statement say what the kit writes and why.
- **Tests.** The panel's browser tests run each case of the rule in Chromium and WebKit with real
  presses, and a WebKit test holds that a pressed button with no tabindex stays unfocused there.
