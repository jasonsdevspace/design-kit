A page can show progress with a known amount, and say it at a pace a screen reader can follow.

- **Sheets.** `dk-progress` draws the native `progress` element as a short track in the line color with its fill in the accent, the bar's pill corners rounding the track's ends and the fill's leading edge square in every engine, through each engine's own parts of the bar, and a bar with no value as an empty track rather than the browser's looping motion. In forced colors the fill takes the highlight on an edged canvas track. The scope test lets the components reach a progress bar's track and fill, and no other part.
- **Controls.** `attachProgress(bar, { status, say, steps, done })` says the bar's name and the words that describe it on a status line, read as each step is crossed, at the steps a page sets, each quarter until it sets others, at least three seconds apart, and once at the end, or the `done` words in its place, which never waits. The amount a bar first shows is not said, new steps are counted afresh, and the end is said once, until the amount goes back for another run. A status line's `say` takes `polite`, for words that give way to a page's own, and progress is said politely, so a page's own result or failure is never put out by it.
- **React.** `Progress` draws the label, the bar and the words under it from `label`, `value`, `max` and `words`, and asks for the words whenever it has a value, since the track is too faint to tell how much is left by color alone. `useProgress()` attaches the bar to the nearest `StatusProvider`'s line, passing new steps or `done` words only when they change.
- **Docs.** The reference has a Progress section, drawn from a new example of an import and a React part written from a file in the examples module, which says how a run that fails partway is shown and said, and the status line's policy names polite words. The README names the new exports.
- **Tests.** Browser tests in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox find the track, the fill and the empty track as drawn, the highlight in forced colors, the label, amount and words read, each quarter and the end said once, three seconds between steps and none before the end, the end of a run that never pauses said, a jump said once, a page's own words said just before kept over progress, the done words, a run that starts again said again, the first amount once known left unsaid, new steps counted afresh, a bar named by its own label with hundredths for words, one taken away saying nothing, the same through `useProgress`, the whole on a 320px phone at twice the text size and under wider spacing, and no audit findings in both themes and in forced colors.
