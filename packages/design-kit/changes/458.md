A page can show progress with a known amount, and say it at a pace a screen reader can follow.

- **Sheets.** `dk-progress` draws the native `progress` element as a short track in the line color with its fill in the accent, on pill corners, through each engine's own parts of the bar, and a bar with no value as an empty track rather than the browser's looping motion. In forced colors the fill takes the highlight on an edged canvas track. The scope test lets the components reach a progress bar's track and fill, and no other part.
- **Controls.** `attachProgress(bar, { status, say, steps })` says the bar's label and the words that describe it on a status line at the steps a page sets, each quarter until it sets others, and once at the end, each at once with no wait to settle. Nothing replaces the end until the amount goes back for another run, and a bar with no amount says nothing.
- **React.** `Progress` draws the label, the bar and the words beside it from `label`, `value`, `max` and `words`, and `useProgress()` attaches the bar to the nearest `StatusProvider`'s line, passing new steps only when they change.
- **Docs.** The reference has a Progress section, drawn from a new example of an import and a React part written from a file in the examples module, and the README names the new exports.
- **Tests.** Browser tests in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox find the track, the fill and the empty track as drawn, the highlight in forced colors, the label, amount and words read, each quarter and the end said once, the end of a run that never pauses said, a jump said once, a run that starts again said again, a bar with no amount and one taken away saying nothing, steps a page sets, the same through `useProgress`, the whole on a 320px phone at twice the text size and under wider spacing, and no audit findings in both themes and in forced colors.
