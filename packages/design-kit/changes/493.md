A refused form shows the kit's errors whatever the page wrote, and focuses a field that can take focus.

- **Upgrading.** A form attached with `attachForm` or `useFormRefusal` now takes `noValidate`, which turns off every check the browser makes on submit, a required field's and a type's, a pattern's and a range's alike. A page that left those to the browser checks each field's `validity` in its submit handler, draws a kit error for each that fails and refuses, as the reference shows, and attaches the form as it is drawn rather than from its submit handler.
- **Controls.** `attachForm` sets `noValidate` on a form it attaches, or on the form around an attached part of one, so the browser's own check no longer stops a submit before the page's handler runs and shows its passing bubble in place of the kit's errors. Fields keep `required`, which `validity` reads. A refusal passes over a control marked off, itself or by a set around it, and where no field in error can take focus it leaves focus where it is and says the first field's error with the count. The form and a group of cells share one check of what can take focus.
- **Docs.** The reference says an attached form takes `noValidate`, that the page checks `validity` itself, with an example, that a form is attached as it is drawn, why `required` stays, and that a refusal passes over inert controls and controls marked off. The field's `mark` comment says why `required` stays.
- **Tests.** Browser tests refuse a form the page wrote with a required field and no `noValidate`, a set in error whose first control is marked off, and a set marked off as a whole.
