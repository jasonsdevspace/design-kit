A busy button shows that it is at work once the wait has passed, not only that it is off.

- **Upgrading.** A page that styles a kit button's own `::after` resets the properties a busy
  button's dots set there, and an element a page places absolutely inside a busy button now sits
  against the button while it is busy. A button whose words wrap stands 0.75rem taller, for the band
  every button now keeps above and below its words.
- **Sheets.** A busy button marked off, once `--dk-wait` has passed, draws three dots centered at
  its foot as its `::after`, a gradient repeated across a box exactly three dots wide, so every
  engine draws three at every text size. They are in the text color and pulse no fainter than 3:1 on
  the kit's grounds, stand still under reduced motion, and keep the system's gray in forced colors,
  where they stand still too. A busy button not yet marked off draws none, so no dots sit on the
  accent's fill. They are centered whatever the button's own alignment, take no place in the row and
  hold no words, so the button keeps its size and its name. Every button keeps a band of 0.375rem
  above and below its words, inside the height of a button of one line, so the dots never meet words
  that wrap or grow. A busy button is `position: relative`, which the dots are placed against.
- **React.** The doc comment on `Button`'s `busy` describes the dots.
- **Docs.** "A busy button" in the reference describes the dots, the properties they set and the
  relative position, and no longer says there is no spinner. How a button is drawn names the band.
  Records 2, 5 and 6 let a busy button follow its `:where()` with `::after`, under record 2's rule
  for a mark a component draws of its own state.
- **Tests.** Browser tests in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox find a busy button past the wait drawing
  the dots, which an off button beside it lacks, with its name kept and neither button's box changed
  by them, the dots centered on a button aligned to its start, three across at larger text sizes,
  clear of words that wrap and of an icon at larger text sizes with every button of one line at its
  least height, out of sight during the wait, no fainter than the pulse's floor, still under reduced
  motion, still and in the system's gray in forced colors, and absent from a busy button not yet
  marked off. A save that ends just after the wait lets real time pass for React's redraw, which
  WebKit needs. A unit test holds the dots at 3:1 on every ground at the faintest of the pulse, and
  the scope test allows the busy button's `::after` alone and holds it to a mark with no words that
  takes no place.
