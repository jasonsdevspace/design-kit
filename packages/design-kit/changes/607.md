A busy button shows that it is at work once the wait has passed, not only that it is off.

- **Sheets.** A busy button, once `--dk-wait` has passed, draws three dots centered at its foot as
  its `::after`, a gradient repeated across a box exactly three dots wide, so every engine draws
  three at every text size. They are in the text color and pulse no fainter than 3:1 on the kit's
  grounds, stand still under reduced motion and keep the system's gray in forced colors. They are
  centered whatever the button's own alignment, take no place in the row and hold no words, so the
  button keeps its size and its name. A busy button is `position: relative`, which the dots are
  placed against.
- **React.** The doc comment on `Button`'s `busy` describes the dots.
- **Docs.** "A busy button" in the reference describes the dots, the properties they set and the
  relative position, and no longer says there is no spinner. Records 2, 5 and 6 let a busy button
  follow its `:where()` with `::after`, under record 2's rule for a mark a component draws of its own
  state.
- **Tests.** Browser tests in Chromium, WebKit and Firefox find a busy button past the wait drawing
  the dots, which an off button beside it lacks, with its name kept and neither button's box changed
  by them, the dots centered on a button aligned to its start, three across at larger text sizes,
  out of sight during the wait, no fainter than the pulse's floor, still under reduced motion and in
  the system's gray in forced colors. A save that ends just after the wait lets real time pass for
  React's redraw, which WebKit needs. The scope test allows the busy button's `::after` alone and
  holds every block on it to a mark with no words that takes no place.
