The kit's words for a failed field name a limit and a step as the field reads them, whatever the
language tag picks and however the attributes are written.

- **Upgrading.** A test that reads the kit's words for a limit or a step expects the new words: a
  year under a thousand written to four digits, as "Enter January 1, 0050 or later.", a limit in the
  Gregorian calendar and Latin digits under a language tag that picks another, and step words
  counted from the browser's step base.
- **Controls.** A dated limit keeps the Gregorian calendar and Latin digits the field takes, while
  the language's own words and order still apply, so `lang="en-u-ca-buddhist"` gives "Enter November
  22, 2026 or earlier." where it gave "Enter November 22, 2569 BE or earlier.", and
  `lang="en-u-nu-arab"` gives Latin digits for a date, a time and a week. A year is written to four
  digits, as the field writes it, so `<input type=month min=0001-01>` is told "Enter January 0001 or
  later." where it was told "Enter January 1 or later.", which read as a day of the month. A number
  field's step words count from the browser's step base, its `min` where that parses, else its
  `value` attribute where that parses, else 0, so `<input type=number step=5 value=2>` is told
  "Enter 2, or 2 plus a multiple of 5." where it was told "Enter a multiple of 5.". The step and its
  base are written as their attributes write them, as a range failure's words write its limit, so
  `min="1.50" step="0.25"` gives "Enter 1.50, or 1.50 plus a multiple of 0.25." beside "Enter 1.50
  or more.", and `step="05"` gives "Enter a multiple of 05.". A `min` that does not parse is left
  out of the words, a step equal to 1, such as `1.0` or `01`, reads "Enter a whole number." where
  the step base is a whole number, and a step that does not parse or is not above 0 is read as the
  default step of 1, as the browser reads it.
- **Docs.** The reference gives the step words and the calendar, digits and year a limit is named
  in, record 13 gives the controls' size with the words, and record 21 names what the unit tests
  find.
- **Tests.** Unit tests find a date, a time and a week limit named in the Gregorian calendar and
  Latin digits under a calendar and a numbering extension, a month in year 1 written so it cannot be
  read as a day, and the step words for a base taken from the value attribute, for a `min` that does
  not parse, for a step of `1.0` and for a step and a base written as their attributes write them.
  Browser tests find the calendar and the digits kept, and the step words counted from the value
  attribute pointing to a value the browser takes.
